01 · Selected research

Research I’m excited about

My research sits at the intersection of visual perception, concept learning, and symbolic reasoning. I develop neuro-symbolic approaches that connect flexible visual representations with structured, programmatic abstractions. The goal is to build AI systems whose reasoning is not only effective, but also interpretable, compositional, and open to inspection and correction.

Much of my work uses challenging visual reasoning tasks, including Bongard Problems, as testbeds for studying how models learn abstractions from examples. More recently, I have been exploring how program synthesis can be used to induce visual concepts from real-world images.

Neuro-symbolic visual reasoning

Combining flexible visual perception with explicit, compositional reasoning. I’m particularly interested in models that can translate what they perceive into structured representations and executable programs, making their reasoning easier to inspect and understand.

Visual reasoningNeuro-symbolic AIConcept learning

Visual concept learning

Studying how machines infer abstract concepts from small sets of positive and negative examples, including Bongard-style visual puzzles, and how those concepts can generalize beyond the examples they were learned from.

Program synthesis

Using program synthesis to discover compact, executable rules that explain visual concepts and model decisions. Programs provide a natural way to make reasoning explicit, revisable, and compositional.
